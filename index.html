Analytical · F1 · aerodynamic & structural assessment

Engineering analysis of Ferrari's rotating rear wing concept that flips 180° for straight-line speed. At ~90° rotation the wing presents maximum frontal area, creating a transient drag spike with strong vortex shedding — a sweeping excitation spectrum that risks crossing structural natural frequencies during braking.

The structural mass needed to survive the oscillatory loads raises the rear CoG, hurting cornering balance. A circuit-specific weapon: 8–10 km/h gain at Monza, a liability at Suzuka.

STAR-CCM+ · transient LES · WMLES (WALE)

Side-by-side LES comparison of two F1 rear-wing DRS concepts: conventional rear-hinged versus Alpine's front-hinged flap. Identical 2-element wing (NACA 4415 + S1223) with overset mesh capturing the opening and closure motion.

Both achieve nearly identical drag reduction, but Alpine's design produces opposing hysteresis — higher lift during closure — favouring predictable transient behaviour and driver confidence over peak DRS gain.

Custom LES subgrid-scale library · Lévêque et al. 2007

Drop-in SGS model for OpenFOAM 10. Subtracts the mean strain rate from the instantaneous one before computing eddy viscosity — naturally damps SGS dissipation near walls without van Driest, recovers standard Smagorinsky in isotropic turbulence, and captures laminar-to-turbulent transition cleanly.

Validated against DNS data for channel flow at Reτ = 395 and 590.

Custom nutWallFunction · Reichardt (1951)

Replaces OpenFOAM's two-layer wall function with Reichardt's single continuous u+ profile — valid from y+ = 0 through the buffer layer into the log region. Newton–Raphson recovers uτ from the implicit profile, νt back-calculated from there.

Drop-in replacement for any RANS or hybrid LES/RANS solver, no buffer-layer discontinuity.

ANSYS CFX · meshing & turbulence model comparison

Compressible RANS over a double ramp (α = 8°, β = 22°) at M = 3.8 — oblique shock formation, shock-shock interaction, shock–boundary-layer coupling. Comparison of inviscid Euler, k-ε and BSL EARSM.

Shock-aligned structured mesh with biexponential node distribution sharply resolved gradients and reduced numerical diffusion versus a universal grid; BSL EARSM with full BL resolution gave the cleanest shock angles.

Solver setup · FFD parameterisation · optimisation pipeline

Gradient-based aerodynamic shape optimisation with two adjoint frameworks: DAFoam (discrete) and adjointShapeOptimisation (continuous). Spalart–Allmaras turbulence, B-spline FFD morphing (8×6×8 control points), snappyHexMesh.

Drag reductions: 1.88% on Ahmed body, ~11% DrivAer (DAFoam), and up to 15.3% DrivAer with the continuous adjoint on a finer mesh.

ANSYS CFX · Euler · analytical validation

2D inviscid Euler simulation of a converging-diverging Laval nozzle. Investigation of choked-throat behaviour, supersonic expansion, and normal shock formation as back pressure varies.

Choked mass flow matched 1D isentropic theory to four decimals (0.035414 vs 0.035417 kg/s); peak Mach reached 3.44 at p₂ = 0.184 bar with a normal shock at exit.

ANSYS CFX · Euler · shock-train analysis

2D inviscid simulation of over- and under-expanded supersonic jets exiting a Laval nozzle (M = 1.5). Captures the self-adjusting oblique shock train as the jet equilibrates with ambient through compression and expansion waves.

Mesh resolution study: fine grid sharpens shock fronts and recovers the analytical jump solution far better than coarse.

ANSYS CFX · Boussinesq · convection cell analysis

Natural convection in 2D periodic and 3D cavity geometries with Boussinesq buoyancy. Sweep across Ra = 500 to 800,000 captures the transition from conduction-dominated to convection-dominated transport.

Nu scales with Ra following the empirical power law; max velocity grows by ~5 orders of magnitude across the sweep, with flow becoming progressively anisotropic.

ANSYS CFX · transient compressible · numerical scheme study

Shock-interface interaction between an acetone–air mixture and SF₆. Misaligned pressure and density gradients generate baroclinic vorticity, rolling the interface into a mushroom shape and dramatically enhancing mixing.

Comparison of Upwind vs High-Resolution advection and 1st vs 2nd-order Backward Euler shows up to 5% peak pressure differences at the shock front and clearly visible diffusive smearing on the lower-order schemes.
